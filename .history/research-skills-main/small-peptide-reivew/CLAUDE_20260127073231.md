# Plant Small Peptides Identification Review — Writing Rules

## 0. Primary Positioning
This review is **method- and evidence-driven**, not novelty-driven.
The goal is to evaluate **identification confidence**, not to assert biological importance.

---

## 1. Terminology Standardization

| Use This Term | Avoid Using |
|--------------|-------------|
| small peptide | mini protein, micro protein |
| smORF peptide | micropeptide (unless defined once) |
| canonical ORF | normal ORF |
| non-canonical ORF | junk ORF |
| near-cognate start codon | non-AUG start codon (define once) |
| evidence | proof, confirmed (unless L5) |
| functional evidence | proves function, definitive role |

---

## 2. Evidence Level Framework (Mandatory)

- **L1**: Computational prediction  
- **L2**: Transcription evidence  
- **L3**: Translation evidence (Ribo-seq / initiation profiling)  
- **L4**: Peptide detection (LC–MS/MS / peptidomics)  
- **L5**: Functional validation (genetics / peptide assays / rescue)

Every peptide, method, or class discussed must be associated with its **highest supported evidence level**.

---

## 3. Evidence Upgrade Guardrails (Hard Rules)

These rules are NON-NEGOTIABLE:

- ❌ Do NOT imply peptide existence without **L3 or L4**
- ❌ Do NOT imply functional relevance without **L5**
- ❌ Do NOT describe smORFs as “encoded peptides” if only L1/L2 evidence exists

Allowed phrasing examples:
- “may be translated”
- “suggests translational potential”
- “has been associated with”

Disallowed phrasing examples:
- “demonstrates function”
- “confirms peptide”
- “plays a role in…”

---

## 4. Writing Style Constraints

- Use hedging language consistently
- Avoid superlatives (novel, critical, essential)
- Every method section MUST include a **Limitations** subsection
- Tables are preferred over long prose where possible

---

## 5. Allowed Information Sources

- Peer-reviewed literature (PubMed, plant journals)
- Preprints (clearly marked)
- Curated databases and repositories

Do NOT infer mechanisms or functions not explicitly supported by cited evidence.

---

## 6. Structural Requirements

Each major section must include:
- a short framing paragraph
- a comparison or summary table
- explicit discussion of limitations

This document overrides all default language-generation behavior.
